The most likely diagnosis is a **WHO Grade 4 astrocytoma (IDH-wildtype)**. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS guideline), which mandates Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype molecular pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS guideline). The tumor demonstrates solid enhancement and significant peritumoral edema, consistent with high-grade glioma behavior. The ependymal contact and cortical involvement further support an aggressive phenotype.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
Based on the integrated analysis and triggered guidelines:

*   **Surgical Oncology:**
    *   Resectability should be assessed, considering the cortical involvement and ependymal contact. Preoperative functional mapping (e.g., awake craniotomy) is strongly recommended due to the location in the dominant hemisphere (right frontal lobe).
    *   Given the multifocal nature and proximity to eloquent cortex, maximal safe resection with consideration for extended resection may be considered, aiming to reduce tumor burden while preserving neurological function.

*   **Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
    *   **RTOG Radiation Oncology Recommendations:**
        *   Conformal Radiation Therapy (CTV) planning must include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to confirmed ependymal contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL guideline).
        *   Cortical involvement necessitates truncation of the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no expansion into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL guideline).
    *   **Treatment Modality:** Stereotactic Radiotherapy (SRS) is explicitly contraindicated as the primary modality due to the tumor volume exceeding 30 mL (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION guideline).
    *   **Adjuvant Therapy:** Standard adjuvant whole-brain radiotherapy (WBRT) or fractionated radiotherapy (FRT) with concurrent temozolomide (TMZ) is recommended following surgical resection, consistent with WHO Grade 4 astrocytoma treatment protocols. Radiation dosimetry should be optimized according to the specified CTV modifications.
