## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging reveals a very large (102.6 mL), multicentric, irregularly shaped mass in the left frontal lobe. The lesion demonstrates solid enhancement with significant heterogeneity (heterogeneity index 0.5125) and a notable component of necrosis (12.45 mL, 12.45% of volume). There is direct contact with the ependymal lining and proximity to the ventricle (5 mm). Cortical involvement is confirmed, and the presence of satellite lesions is noted. The tumor extends bilaterally but is confined to the left hemisphere with no midline crossing.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
The most likely diagnosis is a WHO Grade 4 astrocytoma, IDH-wildtype. This conclusion is directly supported by the presence of necrosis (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS guideline), which mandates Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype molecular pathway.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
*   **Surgical Oncology:** The tumor demonstrates multicentricity and direct ependymal contact, necessitating wide resection. Given its location in the left hemisphere, there is a TRIGGERS SURGICAL WARNING for potential involvement of eloquent cortex. Awake craniotomy with cortical mapping is strongly recommended to preserve critical function and delineate functional boundaries.

*   **Radiation/Medical Oncology:** Per RTOG guidelines:
    *   Radiation therapy requires mandatory CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
    *   Cortical involvement necessitates truncation of the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
    *   Lesion size (>30mm) contraindicates SRS as a primary modality (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION). Conventional fractionated radiotherapy is indicated. RANO guidelines caution against misinterpreting response patterns potentially confounding pseudo-response.
