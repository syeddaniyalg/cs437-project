Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, multicentric mass located in the left frontal lobe, exhibiting solid enhancement. Peritumoral edema is present but focal, with a peritumoral FLAIR ratio of 0. The most likely diagnosis is a high-grade glioma. Based on the presence of necrosis (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS), the tumor is assigned WHO Grade 4. The necrosis forces an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype according to WHO classification rules. While frontal predominance without midline/bilateral crossing raises a prior for IDH-mutant (IDH-MUTANT-PRIOR), the absolute trigger of necrosis in the WHO guidelines dictates the IDH-wildtype pathway.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
*   **Surgical Oncology:** The tumor is located in the dominant hemisphere (left frontal lobe), necessitating awake craniotomy with functional mapping to minimize risk to eloquent cortex (ELOQUENT-CORTEX-LEFT).
*   **Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
    *   Ipsilateral ependymal contact mandates expansion of the Clinical Target Volume (CTV) to include the adjacent ventricular wall (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
    *   Cortical involvement requires truncation of the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no bone/air margin) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
    *   Given the lesion size (>30mm), Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS) as a primary modality is contraindicated (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
