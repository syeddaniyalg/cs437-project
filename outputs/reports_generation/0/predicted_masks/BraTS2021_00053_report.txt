### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, solid-enhancing tumor with a volume of approximately 138 mL, predominantly located in the left frontal lobe. The lesion exhibits ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, measuring 5 mm from the ventricular wall. Cortical involvement is noted, with no midline crossing or bilateral extension. The tumor's shape is highly irregular, with a sphericity index of 0.4162. Necrosis accounts for approximately 21.9% of the lesion's volume.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic and topographical findings, as well as the triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

* Focality: Due to the multicentric nature of the tumor, a gross-total resection may not be possible.
* Eloquent cortex risks: Given the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere, awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk due to the presence of necrosis.
* Molecular prior: The frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing raises IDH-mutant probability (IDH-MUTANT-PRIOR).
* Contraindication: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
