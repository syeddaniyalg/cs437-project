## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe with solid enhancing components and significant necrosis (24.5%). The lesion exhibits multifocal growth patterns and has satellites. Cortical involvement is evident, and the tumor extends to the ventricular wall, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. The presence of necrosis supports this conclusion, as per WHO-GBM-Necrosis guideline.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: Due to the tumor's size and multifocal growth pattern, a subtotal resection is recommended.
* Focality: The lesion's multifocal nature necessitates careful planning for potential residual disease.
* Eloquent cortex risks: Given the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere, awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-Ependymal).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-Cortical).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk: The tumor's necrotic component may lead to a false response to treatment. Close monitoring is essential.
* Contraindications:
	+ SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-Exclusion).
