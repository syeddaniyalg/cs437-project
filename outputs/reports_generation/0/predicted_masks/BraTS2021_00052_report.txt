### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 120.72 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with no significant peritumoral edema or vasogenic edema. The tumor has a high degree of heterogeneity, with an entropy value of 6.4206, indicating a complex and disorganized internal structure. Notably, the lesion shows ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, which warrants CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic features and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that a gross total resection may be challenging, if not impossible.
* Focality: The tumor is multicentric, which may impact the feasibility of a complete resection.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull, without extending into bone or air, due to cortical involvement (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS contraindication: Due to the lesion's size (> 30mm), stereotactic radiosurgery as a primary modality is contraindicated (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
