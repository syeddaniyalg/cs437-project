### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 112.53 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with necrotic components, accounting for 13.79% of its volume. The tumor displays ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall. Additionally, cortical involvement is detected, warranting truncation of the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location necessitate a thorough evaluation to determine resectability.
* Focality: The lesion is multicentric, which may impact surgical planning.
* Eloquent cortex risks: Due to the tumor's presence in the dominant hemisphere, awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk: The presence of necrosis may lead to a pseudo-response, which should be monitored closely.
* Contraindications:
	+ SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
