## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, regular-shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe with solid enhancing features and no significant peritumoral edema or vasogenic edema. The lesion exhibits cortical involvement and has satellites. Ependymal contact is present, warranting CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall. The absence of midline crossing and bilateral involvement suggests a higher likelihood of IDH-mutant pathology.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but further evaluation is necessary to determine the optimal surgical approach.
* Focality: The multicentric nature of the tumor requires careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks during surgery.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk due to the presence of necrosis.
* Molecular Prior: The frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing raises IDH-mutant probability, which may influence treatment decisions.
* Contraindication: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
