### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, solid-enhancing tumor with a large volume of approximately 35.5 mL, primarily located in the right frontal lobe. The lesion exhibits cortical involvement and has satellites. Proximity to the ependymal lining is evident, with a ventricular proximity of 5 mm. The presence of necrosis (30.1%) supports a diagnosis of Grade 4 glioblastoma, consistent with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is WHO Grade 4 Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but further evaluation is necessary to determine eloquent cortex risks.
* Focality: The tumor is multicentric, which may impact surgical planning.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) due to cortical involvement (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS contraindication: Due to the lesion's size (> 30mm), stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) as primary modality is contraindicated (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
