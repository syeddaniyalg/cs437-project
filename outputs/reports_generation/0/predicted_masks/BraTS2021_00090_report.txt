### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 121.47 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with no significant peritumoral edema or mass effect on surrounding structures. Notably, the tumor shows ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, which warrants CTV expansion for radiation planning. Additionally, cortical involvement is detected, necessitating truncation of the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that surgical resection may be feasible, but further evaluation is required to determine the optimal approach.
* Focality: The tumor is multicentric, which may impact the decision-making process for surgical or radiation therapy.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) due to cortical involvement (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS contraindication: Due to the lesion's size (> 30mm), stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) as primary modality is contraindicated (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
