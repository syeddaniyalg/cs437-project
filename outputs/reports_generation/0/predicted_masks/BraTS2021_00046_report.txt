### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, solid-enhancing tumor with a large volume (>30mm) located in the left frontal lobe, exhibiting ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system. The tumor displays cortical involvement and has satellites. Necrosis is present, accounting for approximately 28.71% of the lesion's volume.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but careful planning is necessary to avoid eloquent cortex risks.
* Focality: The lesion is multicentric, which may impact surgical strategy.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk due to the presence of necrosis.
* Molecular prior: The patient's frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing raises IDH-mutant probability (IDH-MUTANT-PRIOR).
* Contraindication: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
* Surgical warning: Tumor in dominant hemisphere; awake craniotomy mapping indicated to preserve speech (ELOQUENT-CORTEX-LEFT).
