### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, solid-enhancing tumor with a volume of approximately 213 mL, primarily located in the left frontal lobe. The tumor exhibits cortical involvement and has a maximum 3D diameter of 114.21 mm. Proximity to the ventricular system is evident, with an ependymal contact distance of 5 mm. The presence of necrosis (23.6%) supports a diagnosis of Grade 4 glioblastoma in the IDH-wildtype pathway.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic and topographical findings, as well as the triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is WHO Grade 4 Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but further evaluation is necessary to determine the optimal surgical approach.
* Focality: The lesion is unifocal, which favors a single surgical procedure.
* Eloquent cortex risks: Due to the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere, awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) due to cortical involvement (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS contraindication: Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm, which applies in this case (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
