### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic and topographical findings, as well as the triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS). The categorical radiomic profile and raw metrics also suggest a highly irregular shape, heterogeneous enhancement pattern, and very large volume, consistent with this diagnosis.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

* Resectability assessment: Given the tumor's size and location, resectability is uncertain.
* Focality: The lesion is unifocal, but its proximity to eloquent cortex in the dominant hemisphere (left) warrants awake craniotomy mapping to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull, without extending into bone or air, due to cortical involvement (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS contraindication: Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30 mm, which this tumor exceeds (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
