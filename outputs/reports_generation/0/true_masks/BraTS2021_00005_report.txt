### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 92mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits heterogeneous enhancement and is characterized by significant necrosis, accounting for approximately 19% of its volume. The tumor displays ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, with a peritumoral flair ratio of 2.04 and edema-to-tumor contrast ratio of 1.60. Cortical involvement is evident, and the lesion does not exhibit any satellites.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but careful planning is required to avoid eloquent cortex risks.
* Focality: The tumor is unifocal, which simplifies surgical planning.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Edema-dominant phenotype creates high pseudo-response risk. Establish baseline before anti-VEGF therapy (RANO-PSEUDORESPONSE).

Note: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION), and awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech due to the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere (ELOQUENT-CORTEX-LEFT).
