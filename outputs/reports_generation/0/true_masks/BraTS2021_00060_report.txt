### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 126.59 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits heterogeneous enhancement and is characterized by significant necrosis, accounting for 19.65% of its volume. The tumor displays ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, with a peritumoral flair ratio of 2.0049 and edema-to-tumor contrast ratio of 1.2039. Cortical involvement is evident, and the lesion has satellites.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS). The categorical radiomic profile also suggests a primary glioma etiology, as ruled out by VASARI-PRIMARY-GLIOMA.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment is crucial due to the tumor's size and location.
* Focality is unifocal, but cortical involvement requires careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks during surgical planning.
* Awake mapping may be necessary if the dominant hemisphere is involved.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* High pseudo-response risk is triggered by the edema-dominant phenotype, necessitating establishment of a baseline before anti-VEGF therapy (RANO-PSEUDORESPONSE).

Note: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
