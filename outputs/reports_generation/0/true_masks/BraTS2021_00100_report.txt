### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 79.74 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with a high degree of heterogeneity and entropy, indicative of an aggressive glioblastoma-like behavior. The presence of necrosis (34.4%) supports a diagnosis of Grade 4 glioblastoma, consistent with the IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. The tumor shows ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma (IDH-wildtype) with necrosis. This conclusion is supported by the WHO Standard guideline "WHO-GBM-NECROSIS" that mandates Grade 4 designation in IDH-wildtype pathway.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**

* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest a complex resection, potentially requiring awake craniotomy mapping to preserve speech.
* Focality: The lesion is unifocal, allowing for a targeted surgical approach.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-Guideline "RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL").
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull, avoiding cortical invasion (RTOG-Guideline "RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL").
* Pseudo-response risk: Establish a baseline before anti-VEGF therapy due to the high edema-dominant phenotype and pseudo-response risk (RANO-Criterion "RANO-PSEUDORESPONSE").

Note: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm, according to RTOG-Guideline "RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION".
