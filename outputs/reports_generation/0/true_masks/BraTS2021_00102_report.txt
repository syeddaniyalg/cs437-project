## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a highly irregular, solid-enhancing tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 38.96 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits moderate volume, with a heterogeneous intensity profile and significant edema surrounding it. Peritumoral flair ratio is elevated at 1.4034, indicating substantial vasogenic edema. The tumor shows ependymal contact, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic and topographical findings, as well as the triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but further evaluation is necessary to determine the optimal surgical approach.
* Focality: The tumor is unifocal, which favors a single surgical procedure.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk due to significant edema and heterogeneity.
* Molecular Prior: The patient's frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing raises the probability of IDH-mutant glioma (IDH-MUTANT-PRIOR).
* Contraindications:
	+ SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
