### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 99.55 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits heterogeneous enhancement and is characterized by significant necrosis, accounting for 23.48% of its volume. The tumor displays ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, with a peritumoral flair ratio of 1.5268 and edema-to-tumor contrast ratio of 0.6489. Cortical involvement is evident, and the lesion has satellites.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS). The categorical radiomic profile also suggests a primary glioma etiology, ruling out other possibilities such as CNS lymphoma, metastasis cluster, or abscess (VASARI-PRIMARY-GLIOMA).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**

* Resectability assessment is crucial due to the tumor's size and location.
* Focality is unifocal, but cortical involvement requires careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks.
* Awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech, given the tumor's presence in the dominant hemisphere (ELOQUENT-CORTEX-LEFT).

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm, which this tumor exceeds (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
