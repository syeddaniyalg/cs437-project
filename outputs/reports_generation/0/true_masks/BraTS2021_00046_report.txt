## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, with a solid enhancing pattern and significant necrosis (22.8%). The tumor exhibits ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, measuring approximately 5mm from the ventricular wall. Cortical involvement is also noted, with a peritumoral flair ratio of 2.8343 and edema-to-tumor contrast ratio of 3.4387. The heterogeneity index is elevated at 0.686, indicating a complex tumor architecture.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection is feasible.
* Focality: The lesion is unifocal, allowing for a targeted surgical approach.
* Eloquent cortex risks: Due to the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere, awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* CTV expansion: Expand the clinical target volume (CTV) to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull, without extending into bone or air (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* Pseudo-response risk: Establish a baseline before initiating anti-VEGF therapy due to the high pseudo-response risk associated with an edema-dominant phenotype (RANO-PSEUDORESPONSE).
* SRS contraindication: Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm, which this tumor exceeds (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
