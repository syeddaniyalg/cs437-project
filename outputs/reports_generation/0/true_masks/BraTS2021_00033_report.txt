## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 110 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing characteristics and is situated near the ependymal lining of the ventricle, with a proximity of 5 mm. The tumor has a unifocal presentation and involves the cortical region. Notably, there is no evidence of necrosis or significant edema.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a high-grade glioma, WHO Grade IV, with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. The presence of ependymal contact and cortical involvement supports this conclusion. Specifically, the RTOG guideline "RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL" recommends CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact, which is consistent with a high-grade glioma.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that surgical resection may be feasible, but further evaluation by a neurosurgeon is necessary to determine the optimal approach.
* Focality: The unifocal presentation of the tumor supports a single-lesion diagnosis.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* Radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion required to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* Radiation therapy planning should consider these modifications to ensure adequate coverage and minimize potential complications.
* SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-Guideline: RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION), so alternative treatment strategies will be explored.
