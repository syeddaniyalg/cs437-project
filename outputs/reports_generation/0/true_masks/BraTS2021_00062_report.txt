### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 85mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with heterogeneous intensity and texture. Proximity to the ependymal wall is evident, prompting CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall. Cortical involvement is also detected, necessitating cortical truncation at the inner table of the skull. The presence of necrosis (1.9%) supports a diagnosis of Grade 4 glioblastoma in the IDH-wildtype pathway.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is WHO Grade 4 Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks is necessary.
* Focality: Unifocal lesion; no bilateral or midline crossing involvement.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion to include ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical truncation at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) due to cortical involvement (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ High pseudo-response risk due to edema-dominant phenotype; establish baseline before anti-VEGF therapy (RANO-PSEUDORESPONSE).

Note: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm, and awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech in the dominant hemisphere.
