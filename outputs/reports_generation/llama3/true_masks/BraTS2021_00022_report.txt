## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 91mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with a high peritumoral Flair ratio of 1.9212 and an edema-to-Tc ratio of 0.7293, indicating significant surrounding edema. The tumor has ependymal contact and is situated 5mm from the ventricular system, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall. Cortical involvement is also detected, warranting cortical truncation at the inner table of the skull.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks is necessary.
* Focality: The lesion is unifocal, which favors a single surgical approach.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk due to significant surrounding edema.
* Molecular prior: The patient's frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing raises IDH-mutant probability, which may influence treatment decisions.

Note: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION), and awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech due to the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere.
