### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the right frontal lobe, measuring approximately 108mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with heterogeneous texture and moderate edema surrounding it. The tumor has ependymal contact and is situated near the ventricular system, with a proximity of 5mm to the ventricle. Cortical involvement is evident, and the lesion is bilateral, extending into the contralateral hemisphere.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation in the IDH-wildtype pathway (WHO-GBM-NECROSIS).

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions
**Surgical Oncology:**
* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and location suggest that a gross total resection may be challenging, if not impossible.
* Focality: The tumor is unifocal, but its bilateral extension requires careful consideration of eloquent cortex risks during surgery.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**
* RTOG_Guideline (RTOG-CTV-EPENDYMAL): CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact.
* RTOG_Guideline (RTOG-CTV-CORTICAL): Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air).
* Contraindication: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-EXCLUSION).
