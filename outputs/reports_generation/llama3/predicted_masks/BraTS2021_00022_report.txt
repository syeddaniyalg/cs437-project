## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor with solid enhancing components in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 118mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, necessitating CTV expansion to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall. Cortical involvement is also detected, prompting cortical truncation at the inner table of the skull. The tumor displays a high degree of heterogeneity, with a heterogeneous index of 0.6462, indicating significant variations in tissue density and texture.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma (GBM) with an IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. The presence of necrosis supports this diagnosis, as per WHO-GBM-Necrosis guideline. The frontal lobe predominance without bilateral or midline crossing raises the probability of an IDH-mutant tumor.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**

* Resectability assessment is crucial due to the lesion's size and location.
* Focality is multicentric, indicating a higher risk of incomplete resection.
* Eloquent cortex risks are present in the dominant hemisphere; awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-Ependymal).
* Cortical involvement detected; cortical truncation at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) is necessary (RTOG-CTV-Cortical).
* SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-Exclusion).
