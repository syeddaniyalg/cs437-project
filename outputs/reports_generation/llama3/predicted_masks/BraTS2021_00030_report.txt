### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor located in the left frontal lobe, measuring approximately 119mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits solid enhancing features with minimal edema and no significant peritumoral flair signal. The ependymal contact is confirmed, and the ventricular proximity is measured at 5mm. The tumor displays cortical involvement and has satellites. The volumetric analysis reveals a large volume of approximately 138mL, with a non-enhancing component accounting for about 23mL.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma (GBM) in the IDH-wildtype pathway. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation according to WHO standards (WHO-GBM-Necrosis). The molecular subtype is predicted to be IDH-wildtype.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**

* Resectability assessment: The tumor's size and location suggest that resection may be feasible, but careful planning is required to avoid eloquent cortex risks.
* Focality: The lesion is multicentric, which may impact the surgical approach.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* Radiation contouring modifications:
	+ CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall due to ependymal proximity/contact (RTOG-CTV-Ependymal).
	+ Cortical involvement detected; truncate CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) (RTOG-CTV-Cortical).
* RANO warnings:
	+ Pseudo-response risk: The large tumor size and cortical involvement may lead to a pseudo-response, which should be monitored closely.
* Molecular prior: IDH-mutant probability is raised due to the frontal predominance without bilateral/midline crossing (IDH-MUTANT-Prior).
* Contraindication: SRS as primary modality is contraindicated for lesions > 30mm (RTOG-SRS-Exclusion).

**Additional Considerations:**

* Awake craniotomy mapping is indicated to preserve speech due to the tumor's location in the dominant hemisphere (Eloquent-Cortex-Left).
