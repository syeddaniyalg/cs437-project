## CLINICAL RADIOMICS & KNOWLEDGE GRAPH REPORT
**Patient ID:** UNKNOWN

### 1. Findings (Radiomic & Topographical Summary)
Imaging demonstrates a large, irregularly shaped tumor with solid enhancing components, measuring approximately 117.76 mm in maximum diameter. The lesion exhibits ependymal contact and proximity to the ventricular system, with a proportion of necrosis estimated at 24.79%. The tumor is multicentric, involving both hemispheres, and has cortical involvement. Peritumoral edema is minimal, with an edema-to-tumor-contrast ratio of 0.0001.

### 2. Integrated Diagnosis (WHO CNS5 Framework)
Based on the radiomic profile and triggered clinical guidelines, the most likely diagnosis is a Grade 4 glioblastoma (GBM) in the IDH-wildtype molecular subtype. This conclusion is supported by the presence of necrosis, which mandates a Grade 4 designation according to WHO-GBM-Necrosis.

### 3. Clinical Management & Prescriptions

**Surgical Oncology:**

* Resectability assessment: The lesion's size and multicentricity suggest that surgical resection may be challenging.
* Focality: Given the cortical involvement, awake mapping techniques should be considered to minimize eloquent cortex risks during surgery.

**Radiation/Medical Oncology:**

* CTV expansion: Due to ependymal proximity/contact, a CTV expansion is required to include the ipsilateral ventricular wall (RTOG-CTV-Ependymal).
* Cortical involvement: Truncate the CTV margin at the inner table of the skull (no margin into bone/air) to avoid unnecessary radiation exposure to healthy brain tissue (RTOG-CTV-Cortical).
* SRS contraindication: Given the lesion's size (> 30mm), SRS as primary modality is contraindicated (RTOG-SRS-Exclusion).
